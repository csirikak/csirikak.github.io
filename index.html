How is color derived from audio?

How Color is Determined:

  1. Audio Input: Sound is captured from your microphone or an uploaded audio file.
  2. FFT Analysis: A Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) converts the audio into its constituent frequencies, showing how much energy (power) is present at each frequency.
  3. Frequency Range Selection: The "Min Frequency" and "Max Frequency" sliders define the specific range of frequencies that will be used for color mapping.
  4. Power Threshold: The "Power Threshold" slider filters out very quiet frequencies. Only frequencies with power above this threshold contribute to the color.
  5. RGB Mapping:
    • The selected frequency range (between Min and Max Freq) is divided into three equal sub-bands.
    • The average power of frequencies in the lowest third of this range determines the intensity of the Red (R) color component.
    • The average power in the middle third determines the Green (G) component.
    • The average power in the highest third determines the Blue (B) component.
  6. Intensity Boost: The resulting RGB values are then slightly boosted in intensity to ensure the color is vibrant and visible, even if the raw audio power in the bands is low.
  7. Display: The final RGB color is displayed in this box (either as a static color or as the newest band in the waterfall view) and as the page's favicon.
